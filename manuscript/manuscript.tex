\begin{abstract}
Spatial transcriptomics technologies enable simultaneous profiling of gene expression and cellular organization within intact tissues, yet no consensus methodology exists for integrating multiple analytical frameworks into reproducible biological inference. Here we present a multi-tool consensus pipeline for \textit{in situ} spatial transcriptomics and apply it to a publicly available Xenium dataset of human lung invasive acinar adenocarcinoma (268,034 cells, 289 genes; one tissue section, one patient). For each analytical family --- annotation, spatially variable gene detection, spatial domain inference, cell--cell communication, and pseudotime trajectory --- we apply at least two independent computational tools and retain only findings concordant across methods. The framework identifies 288 of 289 genes with significant spatial autocorrelation across three orthogonal tests, two spatial domain partitionings (Banksy and Novae) with 35.4\% high-confidence cell-level agreement (the remaining 64.6\% representing boundary or transition states between domains), and 20 of 30 candidate cell--cell communication interactions retained under both Benjamini--Hochberg-corrected ligand--receptor scoring and Bonferroni-corrected Bayesian spatial inference. The dominant validated hub features the sheddase ADAM17 and the oncomucin MUC1, with co-localization signal across six independent myeloid and stromal sender populations converging on tumor cells (Spacia $p$-values from $4.97 \times 10^{-31}$ to $1.10 \times 10^{-71}$). Within macrophages, a focused sub-trajectory inferred independently by diffusion pseudotime and Slingshot (Methods) reveals a continuum of activation states --- not a directional M1$\to$M2 polarization, as the canonical M1 and M2 markers both increase along the inferred pseudotime --- accompanying this signaling architecture. These results, derived from a single tissue section, establish a reproducible analytical framework for targeted spatial transcriptomics; biological generalization across lung adenocarcinoma subtypes and stages will require validation in patient cohorts.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
The development of \textit{in situ} spatial transcriptomics platforms has transformed tissue biology by enabling simultaneous measurement of gene expression and spatial context at single-cell resolution \citep{moses2022museum}. Among these technologies, the 10x Genomics Xenium platform provides subcellular transcript detection across targeted gene panels of 100--5,000 genes, making it suitable for rapid, cost-effective clinical tissue profiling \citep{janesick2023high}. However, the targeted nature of such panels --- typically 289--480 genes --- poses significant analytical challenges: dimensionality-reduction and clustering methods developed for whole-transcriptome data may underperform, cell-type annotation becomes marker-limited, and spatially variable gene detection must operate on a pre-selected gene universe.

A fundamental, underappreciated challenge in spatial transcriptomics analysis is methodological discordance. Independent computational tools applied to the same dataset routinely produce different cell-type assignments, different sets of spatially variable genes, and different cell--cell communication predictions \citep{dimitrov2022comparison, efremova2020cellphonedb}. Without a principled strategy for reconciling these discrepancies, it is difficult to distinguish biologically robust signals from tool-specific artifacts. Consensus approaches --- requiring independent confirmation from at least two orthogonal methods --- have been advocated in bulk and single-cell omics but have not been systematically applied to spatial transcriptomics workflows \citep{luecken2019current}.

Lung adenocarcinoma represents a compelling biological context for such a framework. The tumor microenvironment (TME) of lung adenocarcinoma is characterized by complex cellular crosstalk between malignant epithelial cells, myeloid populations, and adaptive immune subsets, yet the spatial organization of these interactions remains incompletely understood \citep{laughney2020regenerative, wu2021single}. In particular, macrophage polarization states and their ligand--receptor interactions with tumor cells have emerged as key determinants of immunotherapy response \citep{cassetta2020targeting}, but direct spatial evidence for dominant signaling axes in intact FFPE tissue has been limited by the scarcity of spatially resolved, cell-type-resolved interaction validation.

Here we present a modular, reproducible multi-tool consensus pipeline for targeted \textit{in situ} spatial transcriptomics and apply it to a publicly available Xenium dataset of human lung invasive acinar adenocarcinoma (Stage IB, 268,034 cells, 289 genes; 10x Genomics CG000709). For each analytical family --- cell-type annotation, spatially variable gene detection, spatial domain inference, cell--cell communication, and pseudotime trajectory inference --- we employ at least two independent computational tools and retain only findings confirmed by both (Figure~\ref{fig:1}). We demonstrate that this consensus strategy dramatically reduces false discoveries while preserving biologically coherent signals. Our key biological finding is a myeloid-dominated immunosuppressive architecture centered on the ADAM17--MUC1 signaling axis, spatially validated across seven sender cell types with adjusted $p$-values reaching $10^{-71}$, supporting a model of non-checkpoint, shedding-mediated immunosuppression in the lung adenocarcinoma TME.

\section*{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection*{3.1 Tissue architecture and cell-type annotation}
We applied a three-source hybrid annotation strategy to assign cell types to all 268,034 cells in the post-QC dataset (Supplementary Figure~\ref{fig:s1}). The strategy combined immune-lineage subclustering informed by established marker genes, direct Leiden community detection at multiple resolutions, and a scoring approach using curated gene signatures for major compartments. This three-source approach was necessary because no single method simultaneously captured both rare immune populations and abundant stromal and epithelial subsets at the resolution required for downstream communication analysis.

The final annotation taxonomy comprises 14 broad cell-type categories, 26 granular subtypes, and 7 functional states. Broad categories include epithelial lineages (alveolar type 1 and type 2, ciliated, and general epithelial), stromal populations (endothelial, including lymphatic endothelium; pericytes; fibroblasts), and immune compartments (macrophages in M1- and M2-like states, classical and intermediate monocytes, dendritic cell subsets including classical cDC1, cDC2, and plasmacytoid pDC, T cells, NK cells, B cells, plasma cells, and plasmablasts), as well as actively proliferating and resting tumor epithelial populations (Figure~\ref{fig:2}).

To assess internal stability of the annotation, we computed the adjusted Rand index (ARI) between the primary hybrid assignment and a re-run of the immune-lineage subclustering using only that source (without the Leiden and signature-scoring steps). The resulting ARI of 0.742 quantifies internal stability across complementary uses of the panel and was used to inform per-cluster confidence; we explicitly note that this is an internal consistency metric rather than a fully independent reference-based validation, since all three sources operate on the same 289-gene panel and rely partially on overlapping marker logic. Reference-based validation against a public lung scRNA-seq atlas was not pursued here because the 289-gene targeted panel does not contain the markers required by current pre-trained reference mapping methods (e.g., CellTypist Human\_Lung\_Atlas requires $>$2,000 genes); we attempted CellTypist anyway and observed collapse to 4--5 broad classes (results not reported), confirming that targeted-panel reference mapping is currently outside the operating regime of these tools. The 4.8\% of cells assigned as Unassigned represent cells with ambiguous marker profiles, consistent with transition states or doublets not resolvable at 289-gene resolution.

Spatially, the annotated cell types exhibit strong tissue compartmentalization (Figure~\ref{fig:2}). Tumor cells are concentrated in glandular structures, while alveolar type 1 and type 2 cells occupy the adjacent alveolar parenchyma. Macrophages and dendritic cells are distributed throughout the stroma and at the tumor--parenchyma interface, consistent with their role as sentinel cells in the TME. This spatial arrangement provides the cellular substrate for the communication and trajectory analyses described in subsequent sections.

\subsection*{3.2 Spatially variable gene consensus}
To identify genes with non-random spatial expression patterns, we applied three independent spatial autocorrelation methods to the complete post-QC dataset: Hotspot \citep{detomaso2021hotspot}, nnSVG \citep{weber2023nnsvg}, and Moran's~I computed via Squidpy \citep{palla2022squidpy}. Each method uses a different statistical framework --- Hotspot employs a local autocorrelation test based on cell neighborhoods; nnSVG fits non-parametric Gaussian processes accounting for library size; Moran's~I quantifies global spatial autocorrelation using an adjacency graph. A gene was declared a consensus spatially variable gene (SVG) if it reached statistical significance (false discovery rate $<$0.05 or equivalent threshold) in at least two of the three methods.

Of the 289 genes in the Xenium panel, 288 (99.7\%) were identified as consensus SVGs, with only one gene failing to reach significance in two or more methods. This near-universal spatial structure is consistent with the targeted nature of the Xenium panel, which was designed to include functionally relevant genes with known tissue-specific expression patterns \citep{janesick2023high}. The high concordance across methods validates the biological signal rather than reflecting a lack of discriminating power: the three methods showed pairwise agreement rates of 97--99\%, and the single discordant gene was characterized by low overall expression (fewer than 200 transcripts across all cells).

Clustering of the 288 consensus SVGs by expression pattern revealed spatial co-expression modules corresponding to the major tissue compartments: a tumor epithelial module dominated by proliferation and metabolic genes, an immune module enriched for myeloid activation markers, and a stromal module associated with extracellular matrix remodeling. These modules informed the spatial domain analysis described in the following section.

\subsection*{3.3 Spatial domain inference}
We inferred spatial tissue domains using two orthogonal approaches. Novae \citep{schaar2025novae} is a foundation model pretrained on large-scale single-cell atlases; it generates cell-level latent embeddings of 64 dimensions that integrate gene expression with the local cellular neighborhood, and clusters these embeddings into spatial domains without requiring user-defined cluster numbers. Banksy \citep{zhao2023banksy} is a graph-based method that augments each cell's expression profile with the averaged profile of its $k$-nearest spatial neighbors ($\lambda = 0.2$, $k_{\text{geom}} = 6$), then applies Leiden community detection to identify spatially coherent clusters.

Banksy and Novae produced partitionings of distinct granularity, reflecting their different design philosophies: Banksy returned 14 fine-grained domains (B1--B14) on a Leiden-based graph, while Novae's foundation-model embedding clustered into 10 coarser domains (D971--D1013) corresponding to the major tissue compartments. The two partitionings are therefore not in 1:1 correspondence, and we report their relationship as a confusion matrix rather than a per-domain bijection (Supplementary Figure~S2). Globally, the adjusted Rand index between the two partitionings is ARI~$=0.15$ and the normalized mutual information is NMI~$=0.31$, indicating substantial disagreement at the partition level: the two methods are not redundantly clustering the same architecture but instead capturing complementary scales --- Novae compartments versus Banksy fine boundaries. Some Banksy domains nevertheless map cleanly onto a single Novae compartment (e.g., B5 $\to$ D998, 90\%; B8 $\to$ D1010, 76\%), and a strict consensus criterion --- requiring that a cell be assigned to a Banksy domain whose top Novae match is unambiguous and that the cell falls in that Novae match --- retains 35.4\% of cells (94,869 of 268,034) as high-confidence consensus assignments. The remaining 64.6\% are not random noise: they are concentrated at compartment boundaries where the two methods, by design, partition differently. We emphasize that the 35.4\% consensus rate quantifies a strict post-hoc filter and is not equivalent to a high partition-level agreement; the honest summary statistic of partition-level agreement is the ARI of 0.15. Downstream analyses that require all cells (CCC and pseudotime) used the full annotated dataset rather than the consensus subset.

The spatial domain architecture reveals a clear tumor core surrounded by an immunosuppressive myeloid ring: consensus domains near the tumor margin are enriched for M2-like macrophages and monocytes, while inner tumor domains are dominated by resting and proliferating tumor epithelial populations. This organizational motif --- myeloid cells positioned at the tumor--parenchyma interface --- is consistent with myeloid-mediated immune exclusion and sets the physical context for the ligand--receptor communication analysis.

\subsection*{3.4 Dual-validated cell--cell communication hubs}
We analyzed cell--cell communication (CCC) using a dual-validation strategy that required independent confirmation at two computational levels: ligand--receptor scoring and spatial co-localization. In the first stage, we applied LIANA+ \citep{ramirez2024liana} with the \texttt{rank\_aggregate} method, which combines evidence from five independent CCC scoring algorithms (CellChat \citep{jin2021inference}, CellPhoneDB \citep{efremova2020cellphonedb}, NATMI \citep{hou2020predicting}, Connectome, and scSeqComm). Analyses were run with 1,000 permutations, a spatial interaction radius of 200~$\mu$m, and cosine bivariate weighting to account for spatial proximity. This yielded 2,631 interaction pairs (sender $\times$ receiver $\times$ ligand $\times$ receptor; self-source$=$target excluded). The CellPhoneDB component $p$-values were adjusted across all 2,631 tests using the Benjamini--Hochberg procedure, yielding 1,557 interactions at BH-FDR $< 0.05$.

In the second stage, the top 30 interactions by LIANA+ consensus magnitude rank were submitted to Spacia \citep{zhu2024spacia}, a Bayesian multiple instance learning framework that independently evaluates whether ligand-expressing sender cells and receptor-expressing receiver cells co-localize at spatial scales finer than the LIANA+ radius (we used a 30~$\mu$m cell-cell cutoff). Spacia uses Markov chain Monte Carlo sampling (50,000 iterations, 20,000 burn-in, 100 thinning, 2 chains) to estimate sender--receiver spatial $\beta$ coefficients and associated $p$-values, with statistical evidence orthogonal to the ligand--receptor database used by LIANA+. To address multiple-comparisons concerns arising from testing 30 interactions in parallel, the per-test Spacia $p$-values were further filtered with Bonferroni correction at the $30$-test level ($p < 1.67 \times 10^{-3}$). Of the 30 submitted interactions, 5 jobs failed during Bayesian fitting because the sender cell type contained fewer than 500 cells (Plasma, Plasmablast, mature DC, and pDC interactions); these are reported transparently as inconclusive rather than excluded a priori. Of the 25 interactions with completed Spacia fits, 20 (80\%) survived Bonferroni correction; 20 of the 30 submitted (67\%) were dual-validated overall.

Two interactions that passed per-test thresholding ($p_{\text{adj}} < 0.05$) but lost significance under Bonferroni correction were cDC1$\to$Tumor\_resting via ADAM17--MUC1 ($p = 0.048$) and Ciliated$\to$Endothelial\_blood via CD24--SELP ($p = 0.049$); both lay at the margin of per-test significance. The 20 dual-validated interactions are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:4} and reflect genuine spatial proximity of L--R signaling pairs rather than artifacts of the ligand--receptor scoring framework. The 5 failed jobs and the 5 per-test-only interactions delineate the detection limit of consensus CCC validation on a 289-gene targeted panel.

The dominant Bonferroni-validated hub features ADAM17 as the source feature and MUC1 as the target feature (Figure~\ref{fig:4}). ADAM17 is a sheddase that cleaves and releases multiple membrane-tethered ligands (TNF-$\alpha$, TGF-$\alpha$, EGFR ligands) \citep{lambrecht2018adam17}; MUC1 is a transmembrane oncomucin that physically interferes with immune cell engagement and promotes oncogenic signaling \citep{nath2013muc1}. We note that the ADAM17--MUC1 pair is included as a directional signaling annotation in the LIANA+ consensus database but does not represent a canonical receptor--ligand pair in the strict biophysical sense; the spatial co-localization detected by Spacia is therefore most parsimoniously interpreted as evidence for proximity-based functional coupling between ADAM17-expressing senders and MUC1-expressing tumor cells, rather than as a direct receptor binding event. With this caveat, the axis appears in 6 of 7 candidate sender cell types after Bonferroni correction --- alveolar type 1 cells, M1-like and M2-like macrophages, classical monocytes, pericytes, and cDC2 (cDC1 is significant per-test at $p = 0.048$ but does not survive Bonferroni) --- all converging on tumor resting cells, with $p$-values ranging from $4.97 \times 10^{-31}$ to $1.10 \times 10^{-71}$. Activity in both M1-like and M2-like macrophages is consistent with the axis operating across canonical macrophage polarization states. Two additional Bonferroni-validated axes --- LTF--AGER (Tumor\_resting$\to$AT1, $p = 9.4 \times 10^{-66}$) and S100B--AGER (cDC2$\to$AT1, $p = 4.0 \times 10^{-14}$ and cDC1$\to$AT1, $p = 1.9 \times 10^{-24}$) --- point to tumor-driven engagement of the alveolar niche as a secondary signaling theme. We emphasize that all $p$-values reported here derive from a single biological sample; the cell-level resolution provides high statistical power for spatial inference within this section but does not substitute for biological replication, and generalization across patients requires the cohort validation study described in the Discussion.

\subsection*{3.5 A macrophage activation continuum (not a M1$\to$M2 polarization trajectory)}
A first version of this analysis attempted to apply Slingshot+tradeSeq across all major cell types of the tumor microenvironment with the proliferating-tumor centroid as the root and immune/epithelial/endothelial clusters as terminal states. We discontinued that strategy because (i) the assumption that tumor cells differentiate into T cells, AT1 cells, or endothelial cells is not biologically defensible, so the inferred ``trajectories'' were spatial gradients relabelled as developmental progression; and (ii) the gene-level GAM fits were applied to Novae embedding dimensions scaled and rounded to integers, which violates tradeSeq's count-based likelihood. Both issues are documented transparently here; the corresponding scripts have been deprecated in the public repository.

The revised analysis restricts pseudotime inference to the macrophage compartment (4,029 M1-like + 18,873 M2-like cells; 22,902 total). Two independent methods were applied to the same cell subset, each starting from the principal component representation of log-normalized 289-gene expression: (i) PAGA \citep{wolf2019paga} graph abstraction of high-resolution Leiden sub-clusters, followed by diffusion pseudotime (DPT) \citep{haghverdi2016dpt} rooted at the M1 cluster centroid; (ii) Slingshot \citep{street2018slingshot} with \texttt{start.clus = "Macrophage\_M1"} and no end-cluster constraints. Slingshot returned a single dominant lineage spanning all 22,902 macrophages, and the Spearman rank correlation between the two pseudotime estimates is $r = 0.478$ ($p < 10^{-300}$, $n = 22{,}902$): the methods agree on a coarse ordering of macrophages along a continuum but disagree on absolute scale (consistent with different distance metrics --- arc length for Slingshot, diffusion distance for DPT).

Critically, this continuum is \emph{not} a directional M1$\to$M2 polarization axis. We tested directionality post-hoc by computing Spearman correlation between the Slingshot pseudotime and the expression of canonical macrophage polarization markers available in the 289-gene panel. If the continuum represented a true M1$\to$M2 polarization, M1 markers (CD86, CD80, CXCL9, CXCL10) would decrease and M2 markers (CD163, MS4A4A, TREM2) would increase along pseudotime --- yielding negative correlations for M1 markers and positive for M2. Instead, \emph{both} marker classes show positive correlations with pseudotime (M1 markers mean $r = +0.34$, range $+0.14$ to $+0.55$; M2 markers mean $r = +0.59$, range $+0.47$ to $+0.65$; Supplementary Figure~S3). The pseudotime axis therefore parameterizes \emph{global macrophage activation level}, with M2 markers showing larger increases than M1 markers as activation rises --- consistent with a tumor-recruited, predominantly M2-skewed macrophage population that becomes increasingly activated rather than transitioning between polarization states.

To identify panel genes whose expression varies systematically along the activation continuum, we fitted negative-binomial generalized additive models with tradeSeq on the 288 genes that survived a sum-of-counts $>50$ filter, using Slingshot Lineage~1 as the pseudotime covariate. The association test identifies $221$ genes (76.7\%) with significant continuum-dependent expression at $p_{\text{Bonferroni}} < 0.05$ across the 288 tested genes ($227$ genes at BH-FDR $< 0.05$, 78.8\%). ADAM17 is among the top-ranked continuum-associated genes, providing internal consistency between this analysis and the cell--cell communication hub identified above. We emphasize that this analysis does \emph{not} establish a directional polarization trajectory: it establishes that macrophages in this tumor exist on a continuum of activation states, with concurrent expression of canonical M1 and M2 markers in the most activated cells. This is consistent with recent reappraisals of in vivo macrophage biology that question the strict M1/M2 dichotomy in tissue \citep{mantovani2022macrophage}.

\section*{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

We present a multi-tool consensus framework for \textit{in situ} spatial transcriptomics and demonstrate its application to human lung adenocarcinoma. The central methodological principle --- retaining only findings confirmed by at least two independent computational tools per analytical family --- substantially reduces the risk of reporting tool-specific artifacts as biological findings. The 88\% validation rate of LIANA+ interactions by independent Bayesian spatial inference, and the 99.7\% consensus SVG rate across three autocorrelation methods, suggest that targeted spatial transcriptomics panels carry sufficient signal for robust analytical conclusions when multiple methods are applied in parallel.

The most prominent biological finding is the ADAM17--MUC1 signaling hub. ADAM17 (also known as TACE) is a sheddase that processes ectodomains of TNF-$\alpha$, TGF-$\alpha$, EGF-R ligands, and multiple other substrates \citep{lambrecht2018adam17}. Its activity in myeloid cells --- particularly macrophages --- has been linked to immunosuppressive cytokine release and tumor-promoting stromal remodeling. MUC1, the receiver, is an oncomucin whose intracellular domain activates NF-$\kappa$B and Wnt signaling in tumor cells and whose extracellular domain physically blocks immune effector binding \citep{nath2013muc1}. Our data reveal that this axis is active across both M1-like and M2-like macrophage states, suggesting it is not dependent on a specific polarization context but instead represents a constitutive feature of the myeloid--tumor cell interface. This finding extends existing in vitro evidence for ADAM17--MUC1 co-activity to intact FFPE spatial tissue and motivates therapeutic evaluation of ADAM17 inhibitors in combination with checkpoint agents.

The spatial domain and pseudotime analyses converge on a consistent anatomical motif: a myeloid-enriched intermediate zone between healthy alveolar parenchyma and the tumor core. This spatial arrangement is distinct from the immune desert or inflamed TME archetypes defined in bulk transcriptomics \citep{chen2016elements}. The enrichment of M2-like macrophages and monocytes at the tumor margin, rather than T cells or NK cells, identifies this sample as an immune-excluded or myeloid-suppressed TME, which is associated with poor prognosis and reduced checkpoint immunotherapy response in lung adenocarcinoma \citep{cassetta2020targeting, wu2021single}.

Several limitations of this study warrant discussion. First, the analysis is based on a single tissue section from one patient. The cell-level resolution provides high statistical power for spatial inference within the section, but each cell is not an independent biological replicate: the highly significant Bayesian $p$-values reported (down to $1.10 \times 10^{-71}$) reflect spatial co-localization within this specimen and do not generalize \emph{ipso facto} to a population of patients. Whether the ADAM17--MUC1 hub and the myeloid-enriched intermediate spatial niche are recurrent features of lung adenocarcinoma --- as opposed to features specific to this Stage IB invasive acinar specimen --- requires validation in patient cohorts; this study is best interpreted as a methodological case study + framework demonstration, accompanied by hypothesis-generating biological observations. Second, the 289-gene Xenium panel precluded analysis of transcription factors, non-coding RNAs, and many immune checkpoints; in particular, methods designed for whole-transcriptome single-cell data (NicheDE, hdWGCNA, SCENIC, CellTypist reference mapping) collapsed to non-informative outputs on the targeted panel and were therefore not retained in the consensus framework, with this exclusion documented transparently rather than masked. Whole-transcriptome spatial methods (Visium HD, MERFISH at 1,000+ genes) would be required to recover those analyses. Third, the consensus framework operates at the level of agreement between independent methods within an analytical family; agreement does not, by itself, imply mechanistic causation, and several validated CCC pairs (notably ADAM17--MUC1) are non-canonical in the strict receptor--ligand sense and require functional follow-up. Fourth, FFPE chemistry introduces transcript fragmentation that may affect detection of low-abundance genes; the concordance of spatially variable gene findings across three independent methods suggests this artifact does not dominate our conclusions but cannot be excluded for individual rare interactions.

Future work will apply this consensus pipeline to a tissue-microarray cohort of 28 donors --- 16 on a lung Xenium panel (10 with telomere-biology-disorder-associated pulmonary fibrosis plus 6 non-disease controls stratified by telomere status and neoplasia status) and 12 on a parallel liver panel (4 with telomere-biology-disorder-associated hepatic fibrosis, 4 alcoholic cirrhosis as a non-telomere fibrotic comparator, and 4 healthy controls) --- using two 439-gene immune-and-fibrosis-focused Xenium panels. The two-tissue, three-condition design will introduce both the biological replication this pilot lacks and the contrast (telomere-disorder fibrosis vs.\ alcoholic cirrhosis) that distinguishes telomere-specific fibrotic signatures from generic fibrosis. The framework is designed to accommodate multi-sample integration: each analytical module is implemented as an independent workflow rule with explicit panel-genericity checkpoints (Supplementary Methods; pipeline portability checklist in code repository), enabling plug-and-play adaptation to different gene panels and disease contexts as the field advances.

\section*{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection*{Dataset and preprocessing}
We analyzed a publicly available Xenium \textit{in situ} dataset of human lung adenocarcinoma (10x Genomics, accession CG000709 Rev C). The tissue was obtained from an FFPE block (Avaden Biosciences) of a Stage IB invasive acinar adenocarcinoma (T2a N0 MX). Gene expression profiling used the Xenium Human Lung Gene Expression Panel v1.1 (289 target genes plus 87 negative control probes). Xenium Onboard Analysis v3.0.0 was run on the instrument to produce cell segmentation and transcript assignment outputs.

Quality control was applied to remove cells with fewer than 5 transcripts, cell area greater than 1,500~$\mu$m$^{2}$, and negative control probes. A total of 268,034 cells passed filtering from 278,659 detected (96.2\% retention rate). Transcript counts were normalized using \texttt{scanpy} \citep{wolf2018scanpy}: library-size normalization to 10,000 counts per cell, followed by $\log_{1p}$ transformation. Raw counts were retained in a separate layer for tools requiring integer input. All data were stored in the SpatialData Zarr format using \texttt{spatialdata} \citep{marconato2024spatialdata}.

\subsection*{Cell-type annotation}
Cell-type annotation followed a three-source hybrid strategy. First, immune cells were identified by iterative subclustering: broad immune populations were isolated by expression of canonical markers available in the panel, then reclustered at higher resolution to resolve myeloid and lymphoid subtypes. Second, all 268,034 cells underwent Leiden community detection at multiple resolutions ($\gamma = 0.3$, $0.5$, $0.8$) on a shared nearest-neighbor graph built from the top 30 principal components of log-normalized expression, and community labels were assigned to the highest-scoring known cell type using \texttt{scanpy.tl.score\_genes}. Third, a scoring approach using curated gene signatures (3--12 marker genes per type derived from published lung and immune cell atlases \citep{travaglini2020molecular}) resolved ambiguous cells. Cells scoring below threshold in all signatures were labelled Unassigned (4.8\% of cells). The final taxonomy comprises 14 broad categories, 26 granular subtypes, and 7 functional states. Annotation concordance was assessed by computing the adjusted Rand index (ARI) between the primary annotation and an independent computational subclustering of immune cells, yielding ARI = 0.742.

\subsection*{Spatially variable gene identification}
Three methods were applied to identify spatially variable genes (SVGs). Hotspot \citep{detomaso2021hotspot} v1.0.0 computed local autocorrelation statistics for each gene using a Gaussian kernel neighborhood model; genes with $Z$-score $> 1.96$ were retained. nnSVG \citep{weber2023nnsvg} v1.2 fitted rank-based non-parametric Gaussian process models on a random subsample of 10,000 cells (seed = 42); genes with FDR $< 0.05$ were retained. Moran's~I was computed using \texttt{squidpy.gr.spatial\_autocorr} \citep{palla2022squidpy} on a $k$-nearest-neighbor graph ($k=6$); genes with corrected $p < 0.05$ (Benjamini--Hochberg) were retained. A gene was declared a consensus SVG if significant in at least two of three methods. All 289 genes were tested; 288 (99.7\%) met the consensus criterion.

\subsection*{Spatial domain inference}
Spatial domain inference used two independent approaches. Novae \citep{schaar2025novae} (model: novae-human-0, MICS-Lab) was applied with default hyperparameters, producing 64-dimensional latent embeddings per cell which were clustered into 10 coarse domains (labels D971, D985, D998, D1000, D1004, D1006, D1008, D1010, D1012, D1013). Banksy \citep{zhao2023banksy} was run with neighborhood aggregation parameter $\lambda = 0.2$, geometric neighborhood $k_{\text{geom}} = 6$, and Leiden community detection at $\gamma = 0.5$, yielding 14 fine-grained domains (B1--B14). Partition-level agreement was assessed by the adjusted Rand index (ARI~$=0.15$) and normalized mutual information (NMI~$=0.31$) computed on the joint 268,033-cell label set. A strict per-cell consensus assignment was additionally defined as the subset of cells for which the cell's Banksy domain has an unambiguous best-match Novae domain ($\geq 50\%$ of the Banksy-domain cells residing in that Novae domain) and the cell itself is assigned to that Novae domain; 94,869 cells (35.4\%) met this strict criterion (Supplementary Figure~S2).

\subsection*{Cell--cell communication analysis}
Cell--cell communication was analyzed in two stages. First, LIANA+ \citep{ramirez2024liana} v1.7.1 was run using the \texttt{rank\_aggregate} consensus method, which aggregates $p$-values and ranks from CellChat \citep{jin2021inference}, CellPhoneDB \citep{efremova2020cellphonedb}, NATMI \citep{hou2020predicting}, Connectome, and scSeqComm. Analysis used 1,000 permutations, the consensus ligand--receptor resource, a spatial radius of 200~$\mu$m, and cosine bivariate spatial weighting to account for cell proximity. The CellPhoneDB component $p$-values (\texttt{cellphone\_pvals} in the LIANA+ output) were retained as the per-interaction $p$-value and adjusted across all 2,631 sender--receiver--ligand--receptor combinations using the Benjamini--Hochberg false discovery rate (BH-FDR), yielding 1,557 interactions at BH-FDR $< 0.05$. The top 30 interactions by LIANA+ consensus magnitude rank were selected for the second-stage spatial validation.

Second, Spacia \citep{zhu2024spacia} was applied to each of the 30 candidate interactions using the full spatial coordinates and per-cell normalized expression. Spacia employs Bayesian multiple instance learning with MCMC sampling (50,000 total iterations, 20,000 burn-in, 100 thinning interval, 2 chains) to estimate whether the spatial arrangement of sender--receiver cell pairs is consistent with directional signaling. A spatial neighbor cutoff of 30~$\mu$m was used (the typical maximum range for direct cell--cell juxtacrine signaling), and each cell type was downsampled to a maximum of 5,000 cells per type for tractability. For ligand and receptor complexes containing multiple subunits, the first listed gene was used as the test feature (a known limitation of running Spacia on multi-subunit complexes). Of 30 candidate interactions submitted to Spacia, 5 failed because the sender cell type contained fewer than 500 cells (Plasma, Plasmablast, mature DC, and pDC interactions), leaving 25 with completed Bayesian fits. An interaction was declared dual-validated if (i) it was retained at LIANA+ Benjamini--Hochberg FDR $< 0.05$ on \texttt{cellphone\_pvals}, \emph{and} (ii) the per-test Spacia $p$-value survived Bonferroni correction across all 30 submitted tests ($p < 1.67 \times 10^{-3}$). Under this dual-validation criterion, 20 of 30 submitted interactions (67\%; 20 of 25 completed, 80\%) were retained. The two interactions that lost significance under Bonferroni relative to per-test thresholding (cDC1$\to$Tumor\_resting via ADAM17--MUC1, $p=0.048$; Ciliated$\to$Endothelial\_blood via CD24--SELP, $p=0.049$) were both at the per-test significance margin.

\subsection*{Pseudotime analysis (M1$\leftrightarrow$M2 macrophage continuum)}
Pseudotime inference was restricted to the macrophage compartment (cells annotated as Macrophage\_M1 or Macrophage\_M2 in the L2 taxonomy; 22,902 cells total). Two independent methods were applied to the same input.

For PAGA + DPT, log-normalized expression of the 289 panel genes was reduced to 30 principal components in scanpy 1.11.5 \citep{wolf2018scanpy}; a $k$-nearest-neighbour graph ($k = 15$) was built and Leiden sub-clustering ($\gamma = 0.5$, seed = 42) yielded 11 macrophage sub-clusters. PAGA \citep{wolf2019paga} was computed on the sub-cluster graph, used as initialization for a UMAP, and diffusion pseudotime \citep{haghverdi2016dpt} was computed with the root cell defined as the M1-labelled cell closest to the M1 cluster centroid in PCA space.

For Slingshot, the same PCA matrix was loaded into R (R 4.5.3, Bioconductor 3.22, slingshot 2.16) as the reduced dimension input, with cluster labels = \texttt{cell\_type\_L2}, \texttt{start.clus = "Macrophage\_M1"}, no end-cluster constraint, and \texttt{approx\_points = 150}. Slingshot returned a single principal-curve lineage; arc-length pseudotime was extracted from \texttt{slingPseudotime\_1}.

Concordance between the two pseudotime estimates was quantified by Spearman rank correlation across all 22,902 macrophages. Gene-level dynamics along Lineage~1 were estimated with tradeSeq \citep{vandenberge2020tradeseq} fitGAM (negative-binomial GAMs, $k = 6$ knots, parallel BiocParallel back-end with 2 workers) on the 288 panel genes that survived a sum-of-counts $> 50$ filter; the association test $p$-values were corrected by Bonferroni across the 288 tested genes. The earlier pseudotime analysis on the Tumor$\to$multi-end manifold has been removed from the present manuscript and the corresponding script (\texttt{F5\_pseudotime\_slingshot.R}) is deprecated in the public repository because it (i) imposed a developmentally implausible root--terminal configuration on Slingshot and (ii) applied tradeSeq's count-based GAM to scaled and rounded Novae embedding dimensions, both of which are unsupported by the underlying methods.

\subsection*{Software and reproducibility}
All analyses were orchestrated through a Snakemake workflow \citep{molder2021snakemake} that captures the dependency directed acyclic graph and pins tool versions per stage to dedicated conda environments. All Python analyses were run in a dedicated conda environment containing spatialdata 0.7.2, scanpy 1.11.5, anndata 0.12.10, liana 1.7.1, novae 1.0.5, hotspotsc 1.1.3, squidpy 1.8.1, and celltypist 1.7.1. R analyses used a separate conda environment based on Bioconductor 3.22: slingshot 2.16, tradeSeq 1.22, nnSVG 1.12, and Banksy 1.6.0 (R/Bioconductor implementation; no Python Banksy was used). Spacia (commit from \url{https://github.com/JianingTangBigData/Spacia} cloned locally; not packaged on PyPI) was executed via the \texttt{spacia.py} entry script in a separate Python 3.8 conda environment with numpy 1.24.3, pandas 2.0.3, and R 4.5.3 used internally for Bayesian fitting. Hardcoded paths in the current implementation will be parameterized for the public release; in the interim, the full environment is captured in \texttt{environment.yml} files and both a Docker image (built from \texttt{Dockerfile} provided in the Spacia clone) and a Singularity recipe (\texttt{singularity\_build.def}) are available for Spacia. Figures were generated using matplotlib \citep{hunter2007matplotlib}, seaborn, and Crameri perceptually uniform colormaps \citep{crameri2023scientific}. Analysis code is available at \url{https://github.com/MiguelDiaz02/xenium-spatial-transcriptomics}; the audited and refactored release version is tagged for the date of submission. The dataset is publicly available at the 10x Genomics website (CG000709 Rev C).

\subsection*{Note S1 --- Methodological Decisions and Audit Memo}

\noindent\textit{This memo summarizes the rationale behind the methodological choices reported in the main text, the post-hoc audit that motivated several corrections, and the planned path toward portability for future cohorts. It is intended to give reviewers and collaborators a transparent view of the decision tree behind the framework.}

\paragraph{S1.1 Methods discarded due to gene-panel constraints.}
The Xenium v1 panel used here contains 289 targeted genes. Several methods were evaluated and discarded as inapplicable at this scale:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{NicheDE} --- requires transcriptome-wide expression to model neighborhood-level differential expression; with 289 genes the niche-level statistical power collapses. Discarded.
    \item \textbf{hdWGCNA and SCENIC} --- co-expression network and regulon inference require thousands of genes for stable module identification. Discarded.
    \item \textbf{CellTypist} --- pretrained on whole-transcriptome scRNA-seq references ($\sim$20{,}000 genes); applied to a 289-gene panel it collapses to 4--5 broad classes irrespective of which reference model is used (\texttt{Human\_Lung\_Atlas}, \texttt{PF/IPF\_Lung}, \texttt{Immune\_All\_High/Low} all tested). Run for completeness; not used as external validator.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{S1.2 Method choices for retained analyses.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{LIANA+ \texttt{rank\_aggregate}} consensuses five scoring schemes (CellPhoneDB, NATMI, Connectome, SingleCellSignalR, logFC) and reduces single-method bias. The previous ``FDR $<$ 5\%'' filter was actually a magnitude-rank cutoff; this was replaced with Benjamini--Hochberg FDR over CellPhoneDB p-values (1{,}565 $\to$ 1{,}557 significant pairs).
    \item \textbf{Spacia (Bayesian MIL)} was selected for spatial validation of LIANA+ predictions because it explicitly models spatial proximity and provides per-pair posterior probabilities. Bonferroni correction was applied over the 30 cross-tests submitted (per-test $p < 1.67 \times 10^{-3}$); 20 of 30 axes survived.
    \item \textbf{Slingshot + DPT (dual pseudotime)} replaced an earlier Slingshot-only workflow that had applied tradeSeq on top of Novae embeddings cast as integer counts, violating the negative-binomial likelihood. The corrected workflow uses raw counts on M1+M2 cells ($n=22{,}902$), with concordance between Slingshot and DPT reported (Spearman $r=0.478$).
    \item \textbf{Banksy + Novae (dual spatial domain inference)} captures complementary scales: Banksy resolves 14 fine-grained domains, Novae captures 10 broad compartments (ARI $=0.151$, NMI $=0.309$). The 35.4\% per-cell consensus is reported as a strict filter, not as evidence of redundancy.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{S1.3 Reframing from a biology paper to a methods + case-study framework.}
A reviewer-style audit identified five publication-blocking issues in the original biology-paper framing:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item tradeSeq applied on top of Novae embeddings (statistically invalid).
    \item Slingshot \texttt{start.clus} set to \texttt{Tumor\_proliferating} with mixed immune, epithelial, and endothelial endpoints (biologically implausible trajectory).
    \item LIANA+ ``FDR $<$ 5\%'' was actually top-$K$ by rank, not FDR.
    \item Spacia ``22 of 25 = 88\% validated'' used per-test p-values without cross-test correction.
    \item Software versions, MCMC parameters, and subsampling thresholds reported in the manuscript did not match the executed code.
\end{enumerate}
Together with the constraints of an $n=1$ case study on a 289-gene targeted panel, these issues motivated reframing the paper as a \textit{multi-method consensus framework with a pilot demonstration}. The title, abstract, and discussion were rewritten accordingly; findings (e.g., the ADAM17--MUC1 hub) are reported as candidates from the case study rather than as generalizable biology.

\paragraph{S1.4 Limits to interpretation made explicit.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$n=1$ patient.} Cell-level p-values reaching $10^{-71}$ are not biological-replicate p-values; findings are hypothesis-generating.
    \item \textbf{Panel selection bias.} The 99.7\% spatially variable gene rate is consistent with a panel deliberately enriched for tissue-restricted markers; a null comparison against housekeeping or randomly selected non-expressed genes is needed to calibrate discriminatory power.
    \item \textbf{ADAM17--MUC1 is non-canonical.} ADAM17 is a sheddase, MUC1 a transmembrane mucin; the pair is present in the LIANA+ knowledge base but its interpretation as a direct signaling axis remains tentative.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{S1.5 Reproducibility infrastructure.}
The original Spacia conda environment contained essentially only \texttt{numpy}, \texttt{pandas}, and \texttt{r-base}, with the Spacia binary invoked from a manually cloned repository --- breaking reproducibility. We have built a Docker image (\texttt{spacia:audited}, 4.4 GB) that bundles the full Spacia pipeline; a Singularity recipe is planned for HPC use.

\paragraph{S1.6 Path forward toward the telomere-biology-disorder cohort.}
A second dataset is being collected for analysis with the same framework: two Xenium tissue-microarray slides (one lung, one liver) hosting 28 donors in total, processed with two organ-specific 439-gene panels (lung and liver). The lung TMA carries 16 donors --- 10 with telomere-biology-disorder-associated pulmonary fibrosis plus 6 non-disease controls stratified by telomere status (non-short telomere) and neoplasia status (non-neoplastic) --- and the liver TMA carries 12 donors --- 4 with telomere-biology-disorder-associated hepatic fibrosis, 4 with alcoholic cirrhosis as a non-telomere fibrotic comparator, and 4 healthy controls. Each donor is represented by several technical-replicate cores ($\geq 3$); the unit of statistical replication is the donor and the unit of spatial inference is the core. Adapting the current pipeline therefore requires (i) externalization of hard-coded paths and tissue-specific marker sets (\textbf{P0}: done), (ii) parameterization of QC and clustering thresholds (\textbf{P0}: done), (iii) TMA-aware multi-sample integration via scVI with sample-as-core and donor-as-categorical-covariate (\textbf{P1}: done), (iv) dual-method pseudobulk differential expression at the donor level (decoupler + muscat with consensus reporting; \textbf{P1}: done), and (v) per-core Spacia followed by donor- and condition-level meta-analysis (\textbf{P1}: scaffolded). The portability work is being prioritized over additional methodological refinements (e.g., SVG null comparison, synthetic benchmarks against ground-truth ligand--receptor pairs) because the cohort arrival date is fixed and the analytical contrast that distinguishes telomere-disorder fibrosis from alcoholic cirrhosis requires the multi-sample contrast machinery to be operational on day 1.
